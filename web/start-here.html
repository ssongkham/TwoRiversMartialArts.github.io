Start Here

Your first class is free. Just turn up.

There is no trial to book, no form to fill in, and no one will phone you. Find a class on the schedule, arrive ten minutes early, and tell the instructor you are new.

First

Come and watch

Sit at the side of any class on the schedule. Nobody will ask you to join in, and nobody will ask for your details.

Then

Take a class

Most people get far more from one class than from watching three. Wear loose clothes and bare feet. You will sign a release form on the night.

When you are ready

Join

Pay your first month and order a uniform. There is no contract and no joining fee — you can stop whenever you like.

What it costs

Dues are monthly and cover every class at your branch. The more of a family who train, the less each person pays — a family of five pays about $16–17 a head.

What a class is like

An hour, usually. It opens with a bow and a warm-up, then basic techniques, then forms and combinations, and it closes the way it opened. Classes are physically demanding — that is the point — but you work at the level you arrive with, and the instructor will tell you when to sit one out.

Adults and children train in the same class, families included. Sparring comes later, and only with head gear, foot pads and a mouth guard.

We recommend starting at age seven, though there is no upper limit and never has been. If your child is younger, come and talk to an instructor.

Bring on the first night

  • Loose clothing — a t-shirt and sweatpants are right. No uniform needed yet.
  • Water. We train in bare feet, so nothing to put on them.
  • Ten minutes' head start, so you can meet the instructor before the bow-in.
  • A signature on the release form — a parent's, for anyone under eighteen.
